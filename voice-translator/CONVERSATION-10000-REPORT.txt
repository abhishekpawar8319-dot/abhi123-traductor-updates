HOW THE RESULT WAS REACHED
Run 1: 9,951 / 10,000. Found: sentences ending in words like "then", "so", "más" ("Okay then", "Nada más") were held
  and glued to the next speaker's sentence; names lost their capital when a sentence paused before them
  ("lives in houston"). Fixed: only words that can't end a sentence are held; the second piece is kept as heard;
  two pieces clearly in different languages are kept as two sentences (two people).
Run 2: 10,000 / 10,000. Then 5 different mixes: mix 55555 found 1 problem: "¿Dónde está el | supervisor?" was split
  in two because "supervisor" counted as English. Fixed: "supervisor" counts for neither language, and the
  two-speakers rule needs clear evidence (never a single word).
Final: 10 mixes × 10,000 = 100,000 checks, 0 problems. Full suite 249 tests passed on 3 runs.
